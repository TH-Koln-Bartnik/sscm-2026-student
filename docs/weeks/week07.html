Test 4 · what to prepare
  • Backhaus Kaya, Part 5 “The mill question” — the calculations: factor rating, landed cost including the product in transit, one mill or two (the expected cost of an outage)
  • Chapter 11 solved examples — Examples 1–4 (supply chain strategy vs sales strategy, inventory as a share of assets, inventory turnover, weeks of supply) and Solved Problem 11.1
  • Forecast from season and trend (Ch. 4) — the task announced on your Test 3 feedback sheet: a seasonal index and a trend line give the forecast for a given weekday
  • Chapter 11 case studies — the bicycle case, Darden’s Global Supply Chains, Arnold Palmer Hospital’s Supply Chain: work through their questions
  • Calculator allowed; write the calculation path for every calculation
  • Bonus, not in the test: the bullwhip effect (Supplement 11) — why orders swing more the further they travel up the chain, and the cures; the bread chain game shows it

Widget · the aggregate plan builder

Backhaus Kaya’s Christmas quarter from today’s slides, October to January: chase, level and mixed load with one click, or you type your own plan month by month (regular output, overtime, trays bought in). The page counts the ending stock with the rule from the slides, draws baked-so-far against sold-so-far, and prices the season in euros and kg CO₂e. A slider puts a price on a tonne of CO₂: at what price does the cleaner plan win? Three challenges, two of them self-checking. About 15 minutes; start here, then the Colab lab.

Colab · the aggregate planning lab

The worked example of Chapter 13 (Examples 1–4, the roofing supplier in Juárez) rebuilt step by step, with a chart for every step: demand per day against the average, then three ways to absorb the swing — plan 1 level (stock), plan 2 subcontracting (a partner), plan 3 chase (hiring and layoffs) — priced and compared as in Table 13.5. Four short blanks, each with a ✅/❌ check; sliders show when the cheapest plan changes; an optional extra prices the plans in CO₂. About 60 minutes, for revision at home.

  • Open the notebook in Colab → then File → Save a copy in Drive (Tools)
