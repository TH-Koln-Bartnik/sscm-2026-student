Week 5 — Forecasting

Thursday, 29 October 2026 · Heizer Ch. 4 · self-study day

Formatself-study at home: no class on campus today

Group projectbaseline and check cell; checkpoint Wed 4 Nov, 23:59; feedback Thu 5 Nov

Time neededabout 2 hours, in five steps

Every forecast is wrong; a good one misses by little and does not miss in the same direction every time.

How to look into the future?

Professor Bartnik is ill today, so Week 5 is a self-study day. You work through the chapter, one widget and the Backhaus Kaya case at home. The numbers you need to check your case answers appear on this page on Friday, 30 October. Test 3 on Thursday 5 November covers Week 4 (quality) and Week 5 (forecasting).

At the end of this week you can …

  • name the three forecasting horizons and match a decision to each;
  • tell qualitative from quantitative methods, and time-series from associative models;
  • name the four parts of a time series: trend, seasonality, cycles, random variation;
  • compute naive, moving-average, weighted-moving-average and exponential-smoothing forecasts by hand;
  • measure a forecast’s miss with MAD, MSE and MAPE, and compare methods on the same periods;
  • compute weekday (seasonal) indices and a seasonal forecast;
  • use the tracking signal to tell a biased forecast from noise.

Your day in five steps

Week 5 self-study, about 2 hours
Step Time What you do
1 · The puzzle 10 min Read Act 1 of the Kaya case; write down your guesses
2 · Read 45 min Chapter 4 with the reading guide below
3 · Try 15 min The forecast widget: four tabs, no login
4 · Case 45 min Act 2 and Questions Q1–Q6 by hand, with the formula sheet
5 · Exit check 10 min Three short items, then a look back at your guesses

Step 1 · The puzzle (10 min)

Open the case Backhaus Kaya, Part 4: “Eighteen hundred, every night” (Downloads) and read Act 1 only. The bakery delivers 1,800 rolls every night, always split the same way across three stores, and 6 % come back unsold. The prediction box asks three questions (P1–P3). Write your three guesses on a sheet of paper with today’s date and keep it: you check it in Step 5. Ignore the sticky notes and the “we check them at noon” line; they were written for class.

Step 2 · Read Chapter 4 with a guide (45 min)

Read Chapter 4 of Heizer et al. (2023) in the book’s order. Answer each question below in one or two lines on paper while you read. Short answers are enough; they are your notes for Test 3.

  1. Horizons. Which decisions does a short-, medium- and long-range forecast serve? Which horizon is the bakery’s “how many rolls tonight”?
  2. Two families of methods. What separates qualitative from quantitative methods? Name the four qualitative methods and say when you would use one instead of numbers.
  3. Time series vs. associative. What does a time-series model assume about the future, and what does an associative model use instead?
  4. The four parts of a time series. Define trend, seasonality, cycles and random variation, each with one example from your own life (a coffee shop, a gym, a train).
  5. Smoothing the level. How do naive, moving average, weighted moving average and exponential smoothing differ? What happens to the forecast when α moves towards 0, and towards 1?
  6. Measuring the miss. Define MAD, MSE and MAPE. Why does MSE punish one big miss more than several small ones? Why is MAPE the measure to use when you compare two products of very different size?
  7. Trend and season. What does trend-adjusted exponential smoothing add (β)? How is a trend line found by least squares? How is a seasonal index computed, and what do all the indices add up to?
  8. Associative models. What do the correlation coefficient r and r² tell you about a regression line?
  9. Monitoring. What is bias? How is the tracking signal computed, and what does it mean when it leaves its limits?

Then read the Forecasting at Hard Rock Cafe case at the end of the chapter: you know Hard Rock from Test 1.

Step 3 · Try the formulas (15 min)

Open the forecast widget →

It has four tabs; spend three to four minutes on each:

  • Coffee cart: compare naive, 3-day average and the weekday forecast. Which has the smallest MAD, and why?
  • Smoothing: move the α slider. Find the α that reacts to the jump within two days without chasing every random day.
  • Weekday pattern: compute one index by hand, then check it with the calculator.
  • Tracking signal: watch when the signal leaves the ±4 band. Is that a bad week or a stale rule?

Step 4 · The case (45 min)

Read Act 2 and solve the questions by hand on paper, in the fill-in tables of the case, with the Week 5 formula sheet beside you. Write every calculation path. Rounding rules: every forecast is a whole roll, and x.5 rounds up. In exponential smoothing, round each forecast before you compute the next one. MAD and MAPE get two decimals, indices two decimals, the tracking signal one decimal.

  • Do first: Q1 (naive and moving average), Q3 (weekday indices and the week-13 plan), Q5 (Jonas’s Campus rule and the tracking signal).
  • If you have time, or before Test 3: Q2 (exponential smoothing, α 0.2 and 0.5), Q4 (which method wins, MAD and MAPE) and Q6 (three sentences for Aylin, with rolls, euros and CO₂e).

The case is written for a team. If your team can meet online, use the split the case suggests: one pair takes Q1, one pair Q2, then everyone does Q3–Q5. Alone, follow the order above.

Self-check numbers: on this page on Friday, 30 October. Compare your final numbers; if one differs, find the step where it went off before you look at anything else.

Step 5 · Exit check (10 min)

Solve these on paper without the case. There are no answers online: if you are unsure, bring your solution on 5 November.

  1. A bike-rental stand rented 32, 40, 36 and 44 bikes from Monday to Thursday. Forecast Friday with the naive method and with a 3-day moving average.
  2. Last week’s forecast was 200 units and actual demand was 230. With α = 0.3, what is the new exponential-smoothing forecast?
  3. Over four weeks a forecast missed by +12, +8, −4 and +16 units (actual − forecast). Compute RSFE, MAD and the tracking signal. Is the forecast inside ±4? Is it biased?

Finally, take out your Step 1 guesses. Which of P1–P3 would you now answer differently, and why?
