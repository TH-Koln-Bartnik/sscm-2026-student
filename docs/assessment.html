Tests, bonus and code names

What is marked, what earns a bonus, how your papers stay anonymous

Weighting

Module mark = 0.60 × individual tests + 0.15 × team tests + 0.25 × group project. The group share (40 %) is adjusted for each member by the peer evaluation factor. The simulation reports must all pass.
Component Weight When Marked how
Individual tests (four) 60 % Weeks 2, 4, 6, 7, first hour of the morning Pen and paper, anonymous, marked
Team tests (four) 15 % Same mornings, straight after the individual test The same items, answered as a team, marked
Group project 25 % Weeks 2–7; poster session Thu 12 November, afternoon Four parts of 25 points, group mark (see below)
Simulation report required Weeks with a Pearson simulation Handwritten, pass/fail against a scoring matrix handed out in advance
Bonus sheet next test grade 0.3 better Weeks 1–6, 14:10–15:30 Counts or does not count (see below)

Peer evaluation. Your share of the group mark (team tests and project) is multiplied by a peer evaluation factor, as is common in Team-Based Learning: at the end, members rate each other’s contribution. Carried your part → full group mark. Method: see Peer evaluation at the end of this page.

The bonus sheet

Weeks 1–6, end of the afternoon: four to six calculation items, alone, on paper, in class, laptops and phones closed. Every sheet has its own numbers.

  • Upload. Scan the sheet with your phone and upload it to the week’s ILIAS exercise by 15:30, file name = your code name.
  • When it counts. Every item is attempted with the calculation path visible, and more than half of the results are correct.
  • What it earns. Your grade on the next test improves by 0.3 (for example from 2.3 to 2.0), and only if that test is passed. The sheets of all weeks that feed a test count toward that test (table above).
  • Why in class. At home an AI would do the sheet; in the room it measures you.

Handed out in class only; training: the exercises pages.

Your code name

  • A pioneer of operations management with a year, e.g. Babbage1791; handed out on the first Thursday, kept all semester
  • On every bonus sheet and test paper, instead of your name
  • The pairing stays with the instructor; the ILIAS upload shows your account, but the paper is marked anonymously
  • Look your pioneer up: each one changed how work is done

Peer evaluation

Fink’s method (O’Dwyer, 2020):

  • 100 points per student, distributed among the other members of your team, by contribution; equal points are allowed
  • Peer score = the points you receive from your teammates; with equal points it is 100
  • Your multiplier = peer score ÷ 100, applied to your team-test and group-project marks
  • Example: team mark 80, peer score 95 → 80 × 0.95 = 76; peer score 105 → 80 × 1.05 = 84
  • Your points change your teammates’ grades: give them with care
Michaelsen, L. K., Knight, A. B., & Fink, L. D. (Eds.). (2004). Team-based learning: A transformative use of small groups in college teaching. Stylus.
O’Dwyer, B. (2020). Peer evaluation: Fink’s method. InteDashboard blog. https://www.blog.intedashboard.com/blogs/peer-evaluation/finks
